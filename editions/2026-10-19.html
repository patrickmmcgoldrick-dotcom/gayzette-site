A 501-billion-parameter coding model, free to download, with a permissive license

Reflection's Beam is its first open-weight model, and you can try it today through Ollama or OpenRouter.

By The Gayzette, with Claude · 2 min read · Wonder ◆◆◆◆◇

Reflection, an AI lab, published the weights of a coding model called Beam on Hugging Face on October 16, under the Apache 2.0 license, which lets you use, change and sell things built on it. "Open-weight" means the finished model file is yours to download, like being handed the baked cake instead of just a slice at a restaurant. It is Reflection's first.

Beam is a sparse mixture-of-experts model. It has 501 billion parameters (the adjustable dials set during training) but uses only 23 billion on any given step, a bit like a big writers' room where only the few writers who know the subject speak up on each joke. It targets coding, reasoning and long-running agent tasks. Weights come in three number formats (BF16, FP8 and NVFP4, each progressively more compact), plus a helper "drafter" model for speed. You can reach it through Reflection's API (free to start, in public beta), OpenRouter, Ollama, AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft Foundry, Together AI and Fireworks.

The caveat: this is the vendor's own announcement. The post gives no benchmark numbers (it points to a separate model card) and no minimum hardware, and the release is about three days old, so treat the performance story as unproven until others test it.

On your desk: Patrick, try Beam through OpenRouter or Ollama as the model behind one of your n8n workflows, and see whether a coding model with no vendor lock-in can do a job your Claude Code routines handle today.

AI

Meta releases Muse Spark Open, an 816B model with 1M-token memory

It reportedly runs on two Mac Studios, but read the license before building a product on it.

Meta released Muse Spark Open, an open-weight model with 816 billion parameters in total and 42 billion active at a time. It handles images natively and has a 1-million-token context window, which is how much text it can hold in mind at once. Meta calls it the most capable US open-weight model so far; that is Meta's own claim, with no benchmark figures listed.

The license is the Muse Model License, which has limited commercial terms, so it is not Apache-style free-for-anything. Meta says the model runs on two Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios (512 GB of shared memory each) using MLX, Apple's machine-learning toolkit. Hosted versions are on Baseten, Fireworks, Groq, Modal, Nebius, OpenRouter and Together AI; pricing is not stated.

On your desk: Patrick, a 1M-token window on a pair of Macs makes it conceivable to process your whole Obsidian vault locally, though you would want to check the license terms first.

More in AI

NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5: a 120B vision model and a 24GB one

Read the brief

The smaller model squeezes into the memory of a single consumer graphics card.

NVIDIA released two models on October 15. Nemotron 3.5 Super VL is an open 120-billion-parameter model that reads images as well as text; it is on Hugging Face, OpenRouter, ModelScope and as an NVIDIA NIM package. Nemotron 3.5 Bolt (74B-A9B) uses native 2-bit quantization, storing each number with very few bits, like shrinking a photo file, so it fits in 24GB of memory. It runs through llama.cpp, Ollama, LM Studio, Unsloth and vLLM.

A 4GB Bolt E4B edge model is announced for October 23 and is not out yet. NVIDIA also says it is releasing open datasets and training environments. The page names no license, and the speed claims (up to 5x faster throughput than other models in its class, per NVIDIA's chart) are NVIDIA's own.

On your desk: Patrick, Bolt in Ollama or LM Studio is a cheap way to test a local helper for your scheduled vault routines, with nothing leaving your machine.

xAI's Grok Voice 3.0 costs $0.08 a minute

Read the brief

A speech-to-speech model for building phone-style voice agents, with a price unchanged from 2.0.

xAI released Grok Voice 3.0, a speech-to-speech model: audio goes in, audio comes out, with no separate text step in between. It sits in xAI's Voice Agent API, and the grok-voice-latest alias switched over automatically. The price is $0.08 a minute ($4.80 an hour), the same as version 2.0.

xAI cites Artificial Analysis scores: 86.9 on its Speech-to-Speech Index (versus 83.2 for GPT-Live-1) and 98.5% on Big Bench Audio. On the tau-Voice agent test it scored 71.6%, slightly below GPT-Live-1's 74.5%. xAI also says Starlink call centers handle 20,000 calls a day with about 70% resolved without a human. All of these numbers are vendor-reported.

Spirit

Two meditation styles lowered depression scores in a French trial

Self-inquiry did not beat plain focused attention, which is the opposite of what the authors predicted.

Céline Stinus and Sophie Berjot randomized 147 French adults (49 per group) to focused-attention meditation (resting attention on one object, like the breath), self-inquiry meditation (examining thoughts, beliefs and the sense of self) and a wait-list. Each meditation group had five 60-minute online sessions over four weeks plus 20 minutes of individual practice five days a week (Mindfulness journal, via PsyPost).

Both meditation groups scored lower on depressive symptoms and identity threat than the wait-list; the two styles did not reliably differ. Only focused attention reduced dysfunctional attitudes, contrary to the authors' expectation that self-inquiry would. Caveats: participants were nonclinical with low starting scores, all outcomes were self-reported, one instructor taught both, it was not preregistered, and wait-list dropout was 20 people versus 4 to 5 per meditation group. Research reporting, not health advice.

On your desk: Patrick, self-inquiry is the practice closest to your consciousness beat, and a prediction that failed in the open fits the honest-scoring habit you bring to your remote viewing log.

More in Spirit

Compass Pathways plans to finish its psilocybin FDA filing this quarter

Read the brief

The company hopes for a launch in early 2027; the drug is not approved.

At the STAT Summit in Boston, Compass Pathways CEO Kabir Nath said the company will "go slow before we go fast." Compass plans to complete its rolling FDA submission for COMP360, a synthetic psilocybin for treatment-resistant depression, in the fourth quarter of 2026. It hopes to launch in early 2027. COMP360 is not approved; Nath expects a REMS (an FDA-required safety program) if it is.

Per STAT, about 25% of Phase 3 patients had a meaningful response after one roughly six-hour session, and about 40% after a second dose. Harvard's Steven Hyman cautioned that blinding remains a problem (people can often tell they got the drug) and that it is unclear whether the psychedelic experience itself is needed for any benefit. These are company figures, not FDA findings; this is regulatory news, not health advice.

On your desk: Patrick, Hyman's blinding point is the same measurement-honesty question you ask of your remote viewing scoring: how do you know the result is not expectation?
