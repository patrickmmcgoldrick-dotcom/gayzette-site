Claude API now shows its work in tracing tools

OpenTelemetry export, in beta, covers Messages API requests and Managed Agents sessions.

The Gayzette · Wonder ◆◆◇◇◇

Anthropic has launched OpenTelemetry export in beta for the Claude API. OpenTelemetry is a common format for recording what a program did, step by step (each step is called a span), so any tracing tool can display it. The October 14 release notes say it covers Messages API requests and Claude Managed Agents sessions.

The Claude SDK emits spans in Python, TypeScript, C#, Go, Java, PHP and Ruby. To get spans from the API itself, you add an OpenTelemetry destination in the Claude Console. The same day, Managed Agents scheduled deployments became available on Claude Platform on AWS.

This is developer plumbing, not a headline launch, but plumbing is exactly what you want when something breaks at 6 a.m. and nobody is awake to explain it.

On your desk: when an n8n workflow that calls Claude misbehaves, a trace lets you see what each call did instead of guessing. Try it on your busiest automation first.

Claude Code tightens hooks and adds usage warnings

Versions 2.1.299 and 2.1.300 both landed October 14.

Claude Code 2.1.299 adds a setting, CLAUDE_CODE_DISABLE_FILE_MENTIONS, so @ file mentions pass the file's path for Claude to read instead of pasting its contents into your message. UserPromptSubmit hooks (small scripts that run when you send a prompt) now run before a skill or slash command expands, so a hook that blocks a prompt also stops the shell commands embedded in it. And /usage now warns about max effort, workflows, always-on context and large tool results.

Version 2.1.300 adds /advisor on Claude Platform on AWS and splits OpenTelemetry cache-write counts into 5-minute and 1-hour tokens. Hook updatedInput from PreToolUse and PermissionRequest is now validated: unknown keys are dropped and rejected input denies the call. Claude Tag in Slack gains Slack Connect channel support (off until an Owner or admin enables it). On your desk: re-test any skill hook that rewrites tool input, and glance at /usage before your next long session.

More in AI

Gemini API deprecates two more Flash models

Old requests get auto-routed, so nothing errors, but the model underneath changes.

Read the brief

Google's Gemini API changelog lists two deprecations on October 8. The gemini-3.7-flash model is deprecated and replaced by gemini-3.8-flash, with old requests automatically routed to the new model. Likewise gemini-3.5-flash is deprecated, with requests routed to gemini-3.6-flash, and the changelog says to update your model strings. "Deprecated" means the vendor is retiring a model; auto-routing means your calls keep working but land on a different model.

A related October 6 entry deprecates gemini-3.1-flash-image, with no shutdown date announced. A different model answering under an old name can change your outputs without any error to warn you. On your desk: search your n8n Gemini nodes and scripts for those three model names and repoint them on purpose, so the swap happens when you choose and not when Google does.

Spirit

Brain "entropy" in a psilocybin trip tracked later insight

A small, exploratory Imperial College study, reported by PsyPost.

This comes from PsyPost's report on a Nature Communications paper from Imperial College London; we read the report, not the paper. Twenty-eight healthy adults who had never used a psychedelic took 1 mg (placebo-like) and then, a month later, 25 mg of psilocybin, always in that order. Brain entropy, a measure of how unpredictable the EEG signal is, rose sharply after 25 mg. Insight scores were about 51 points higher the next day (0-100 scale) and stayed elevated a month later.

The caveats are big: fixed dose order, no randomization, a small group, and the study is described as highly exploratory. Outside expert Katrin Preller noted that "one month after psilocybin administration only very marginal changes were detected," and Martin Korsbak Madsen said "psychedelics can have long-term effects but underlying mechanisms are unknown." On your desk: it is a nice prompt for a Universe Year note on what "expanded awareness" can be measured as, and a good reminder to teach it as a hypothesis.

More in Spirit

Low-dose LSD sharpened attention in women, not men, in a pilot

A conference presentation, not a published paper, reported by The Debrief.

Read the brief

The Debrief reports a presentation at the ECNP congress in Munich; no journal paper or DOI is cited, so treat this as early. Researchers from the University of Southern Denmark and Maastricht University gave 48 healthy volunteers (24 men, 24 women) four doses of 15 micrograms of LSD or placebo over two weeks, double-blind. That dose is about a quarter of the 60 micrograms typically associated with a full LSD experience.

Women on LSD had faster reaction times (335 to 308 milliseconds, about 8%) and fewer attention lapses (2.3 versus 4.7 on placebo). Men showed no comparable improvement on any measure. The authors say this does not show low-dose LSD improves thinking in general, and the group was small. On your desk: a useful example for your meditation notes of why "it worked for some people" is not the same as "it works," and why small samples deserve a raised eyebrow.
