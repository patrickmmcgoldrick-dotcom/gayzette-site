Claude Code mods: tiny TypeScript functions that reshape the tool

Anthropic's new "mods" let you intercept what Claude Code does and rewrite it, add buttons, or add guardrails, mid-session.

By The Gayzette, with Claude · 2 min read · Wonder ◆◆◆◆◇

Anthropic launched "mods" on Oct. 1: small TypeScript functions (TypeScript is a popular web programming language) that change how Claude Code behaves. Per the announcement, a mod can "rewrite a prompt, add new UI, replace a built-in feature, or add entirely new functionality." They are available today in the Claude Code CLI and desktop app.

The trick is events. Every Claude Code action sends out an event, a little signal a mod can catch. A mod can edit a prompt before it reaches the model, block or rewrite a tool call, handle a permission request, or change parts of the interface. Examples include redacting secrets from tool output and adding custom buttons. Mods from different creators stack, and they hot-reload, meaning they update mid-session. Install them through plugins from the Claude directory or with /plugin.

The fine print: mods are not sandboxed, so treat each like software with real reach. Enterprise accounts get a built-in sec-default mod that stops user mods from overriding permission rules.

On your desk: your Gayzette and Stacy Show offices both run on Claude Code and Cowork. A small mod that logs each tool call into the day's handoff note, or one that redacts secrets from tool output before you paste a transcript into a Skool lesson, is a few lines of TypeScript.

The tool you already live in can now be bent to your workflow with a few lines of code, including guardrails and auto-logging.

Suno Speech (beta) makes voice and music in one track

Suno, the AI music maker you already build with, introduced Suno Speech in beta on Oct. 1. You give it written text plus a chosen voice style and music style, and it generates the spoken voice and original music together as one track. Suno's suggested uses include dramatic readings, voice notes, meditations, poems, pep talks and personalized storytelling.

It went out to the whole Suno community after limited testing. The page is frank about the state of things: it warns that British accents can wander off to Australia and back, and that dramatic pauses may be very dramatic. No pricing or usage numbers are given.

On your desk: a scored voice-over for a Travis Wall Studio class intro, or a guided-meditation track for your own practice or a Skool course, could come out in one pass. For the Stacy Show, try a mock-dramatic reading as a quick audio bit, pauses and all.

Spirit

Seven-day meditation retreat shifted brain and blood markers

A quiet window for spirit news, so this one is about eight weeks old (ScienceDaily, early August). UC San Diego researchers followed 20 healthy adults through a seven-day intensive meditation retreat. Brain activity showed less internal mental chatter in meditation-related regions.

Lab neurons exposed to post-retreat blood plasma grew longer branches and more connections, and blood endogenous opioids (the body's own pain-and-pleasure chemicals) rose. Mystical Experience Questionnaire scores went from 2.37 to 3.02. Connectivity patterns resembled those seen in psychedelic experiences, with no drug involved. Limits: healthy participants only, and the retreat's parts could not be separated.

On your desk: a skeptic-friendly data point for your meditation practice, and a nice sidebar for the meditation and higher-self material in your Skool courses. Twenty people is small, so say so in the lesson.

More in Spirit

USC trial asks if mindfulness boosts psilocybin therapy

Read the brief

Also older than usual: this is from June (The Debrief). An open-label trial at the University of Southern California is enrolling about 72 adults new to both psychedelics and meditation. One group gets psilocybin alone, the other gets psilocybin plus eight weeks of mindfulness training.

It is funded by ARPA-H's EVIDENT initiative and collects EEG, MRI and fMRI scans and biological samples at baseline and at 3, 6 and 12 months. Director Dr. Rael Cahn says mindfulness meditation gives people tools to work with unhelpful thought patterns that psychedelic therapy might otherwise amplify. This describes the design only; there are no results yet.

On your desk: it is a controlled test of the meditation-meets-psychedelics idea from your Dispenza and Hawkins listening. Worth bookmarking for the day results arrive, and for fact-checking any course claims on the topic.
