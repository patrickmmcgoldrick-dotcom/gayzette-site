Google's new free search model fits on a laptop

EmbeddingGemma 2 lets a small, free model search text, code, images, video and audio by meaning.

Google released EmbeddingGemma 2 on October 6. An embedding model turns a piece of content into a list of numbers, so things with similar meaning end up with similar numbers. That is what lets a computer search by meaning instead of exact words. This one puts text, code, images, video and audio into the same shared list format, so a sentence can find a matching picture or clip.

The size is the news. The full model has 740M parameters (the adjustable settings inside a model), under an Apache 2.0 license, which is an open license anyone can read. Text-only use needs as few as 270M parameters; the vision (170M) and audio (300M) parts are optional add-ons. With quantization, which means storing the model's numbers in a smaller form, the text-only weights use about 191MB of memory on a Pixel 11 Pro phone. Each item's list can also be trimmed from 768 numbers to 512, 256 or 128, for up to 6x less storage.

Google also reports its code-search score on MTEB Code, a standard benchmark, rose from 68.76 to 78.68. Caveat: that is Google's own announcement, and we have not seen outside tests. The weights are on Hugging Face and Kaggle and work with Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp, sentence-transformers and Qdrant.

On your desk: this is the missing piece for searching your Obsidian vault by meaning, including images and the dance-class audio and video, with no API bill because it runs on your own machine. Worth an afternoon of trying it on a small folder first.

On your desk A search tool that small and free means your own notes and class recordings can become searchable without paying anyone per query.

Google cuts Nano Banana image prices roughly in half

Google's image maker got a new version, Nano Banana 2.1, on October 7. A 1K image (about 1,000 pixels on a side) now costs $0.0336, down from $0.067, and a 4K image costs $0.0756, down from $0.151. It is built on Gemini 3.6 Flash, takes up to 14 reference images per prompt, and has minimal, medium and high thinking levels. It is rolling out in the Gemini app, AI Studio, Flow, Stitch and Google Ads.

Two caveats. Google says the pricier Nano Banana Pro still "often" makes noticeably better images, at $0.134 per 1K image. And the new API drops function calling, structured outputs, caching and Live API support the older one had. This comes from AI Weekly citing The Decoder, a secondary source; we did not open the original.

On your desk: cheap batch images for Squarespace pages, Skool course thumbnails and Travis Wall Studio promos, called from n8n. Check whether your workflows lean on any of the dropped features first.

Anthropic widens its Claude Startups program

Anthropic expanded its Claude Startups program on October 6. Companies new to Claude Team get one free year for up to five Premium seats (about $6,000, per the page) plus a one-time $1,000 API credit once approved. There are also partner offers worth up to $45,000 combined, virtual office hours with Anthropic's Applied AI team, and help building and submitting a connector or plugin to the Claude Marketplace.

Eligibility: founded in the last five years or funded in the last two. Read the page's fine print before counting on any of it.

OpenAI tests computer-use agents on contract-review work

OpenAI and Ironclad, a contract software company, built 11 research tasks across legal, commercial and procurement work, each scored against 8 to 50 criteria. A computer-use agent is an AI that operates software the way a person would, by looking at the screen and clicking. GPT-6 Astra (Max reasoning) averaged 55.0%, against 41.6% for GPT-5.6 Sol (High reasoning). An internal model scored 63.7%.

Estimated time per attempt fell from 37.0 to 19.2 minutes, but the page says those are simulated estimates, not measured. Training used synthetic tasks built from public SEC EDGAR contracts. There is no product or API release; OpenAI invites software companies to apply for a research collaboration.

On your desk: roughly 55% on messy multi-step office work is a good lesson in why a human should still read contracts and vendor paperwork for the studio and the memoir.

Plain note first: this is mid-September news (the Psychiatric Times byline says 2026-09-15, though the page data conflicts), run today because nothing newer cleared the bar this week. The hearing followed an April 2026 executive order, finalized FDA guidance on clinical investigations, and 3 national priority vouchers for sponsors studying psilocybin and methylone.

Compass Pathways' COMP360 has durability data through 6 months in treatment-resistant depression and is in a rolling FDA submission, meaning it sends the paperwork in pieces. Definium's DT120 completed its third positive phase 3 readout, with a pre-NDA meeting expected in late 2026. A JAMA Psychiatry meta-analysis cited at the hearing found serious adverse events in roughly 4% of trial participants with pre-existing psychiatric conditions. Presenters disagreed on the road to approval.

  • 01Stanford molecule makes lymphoma tumors vanish in mice Wed Oct 7

    TCIP3 is a two-part molecule aimed at BCL6, a protein in diffuse large B-cell lymphoma, the most common non-Hodgkin lymphoma. In mice, "By 11 days, the tumors that had been treated with TCIP3 were completely gone," with no obvious toxicity (Cell, 2026). Caveat: mice, and it is "not yet ready for use in people."

    ScienceDaily (Stanford Medicine)

  • 02CRISPR builds cancer-fighting CAR-T cells inside the body Wed Oct 7

    UCSF's Justin Eyquem and colleagues used two injected particles to engineer CAR-T cells (immune cells trained to hunt cancer) inside the body. Today's versions typically cost $400,000 to $500,000 because cells are removed, engineered and put back. In mice with humanized immune systems, one injection cleared detectable leukemia in nearly all animals within two weeks (Nature, 2026); it still needs clinical trials.

    ScienceDaily (UCSF)

  • 03Saudi solar park becomes a refuge for threatened sand gazelles Wed Oct 7

    A 250-megawatt solar plant serving the Amaala resort sits on a fenced 1,200-acre site inside a 24,500-square-mile nature reserve. Gazelles at the site now number 38; about 3,000 Arabian sand gazelles are left in the wild, and the goal is of more than 500 before reintroduction. The panels' shade runs 8 to 18 degrees cooler.

    Good News Network

  • 04Strangers rebuild shelter for Kansas man after televised plea Wed Oct 7
