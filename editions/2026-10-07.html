OpenAI shares new math results many with Lean-checked proofs

An unreleased internal model produced a batch of results, and many of the proofs come formalized in Lean, a language that lets a computer check every step.

OpenAI published a broad set of new mathematical results from an internal model it has not released, posting them in a public GitHub repository. Many of the proofs are formalized in Lean, a proof-checking language: think of it as a spellchecker for logic, where a computer confirms each step follows from the last, so nobody has to take the model's word for it.

OpenAI says the average result took compute equal to about three hours of ChatGPT Pro usage. The repository also includes 10 summaries of the model's reasoning and statistics on the problems it attempted, not only the ones it solved. The company consulted the Institute for Advanced Study's Advisory Group on Mathematics and AI, and says it plans to release the model responsibly.

Worth knowing: a secondary report citing Scientific American gives a specific total count of results, but we have not confirmed it at OpenAI's own page, so we are leaving it out. Only many, not all, of the proofs have Lean formalizations so far, so treat the rest as claims until they are checked.

On your desk: for your Universe Year math-and-biology study, the Lean repo is a real-world example of what it looks like when a claim comes with a checkable proof, which is a good yardstick for scoring your own remote-viewing hits the same way: write the claim first, check it after.

On your desk It shows whether AI math claims can come with proofs a computer can check, rather than asking for trust.

Mistral previews Large 4, with weights promised for late October

Mistral announced Large 4, a model that handles text and images. It has 1 trillion parameters in total (the adjustable numbers that make up a model), with 49 billion active at a time, and supports 160+ languages.

A public preview is live now through Mistral's API at $1.36 per million input tokens and $4.18 per million output tokens (a token is roughly three-quarters of a word). Mistral says the weights, the downloadable files that let you run it yourself, will arrive at the end of October. They are not out yet, and the license was not stated on the page we read, so "open" remains a promise.

Mistral also claims 82% on a vulnerability reproduce-and-patch test in the Artificial Analysis Cyber Index, which Mistral calls the highest of any model; that is the vendor's own claim.

On your desk: when the weights ship, Large 4 is a candidate to swap into one classify-and-route step in your n8n automations as a cost test against what you run now. Treat it as a model to try, not a replacement.

Anthropic sorts its security-testing access into three tiers

Anthropic folded Project Glasswing into a Cyber Verification Program with three tiers: Defense Access, Red Team Access (for organizations only) and Specialized Access. It covers Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Mythos 5.1 and future models. Think of it as a badge system: verified security people get fewer refusals on work that looks risky to an unverified stranger.

Anthropic reports that Glasswing partners found over 129,000 verified vulnerabilities from April to July 2026, with more than 33,000 rated critical or high. On its CyScenarioBench test, Opus 5.5 completed 34 of 50 tasks in the Red Team tier with no blocks, while 46 of 50 tasks were blocked in the Defense tier. These are Anthropic's own figures.

Applications go through Anthropic's portal.

On your desk: this explains why Claude may balk at security-flavored prompts when you are hardening your Squarespace site or Travis Wall Studio's Skool setup, and it tells you a verified route exists if you ever need one.

Meta and Sierra publish a protocol for shoppers' AI agents

Sierra and Meta are leading an open standard, the Personal Agent Protocol, for how a consumer's own AI agent signs in with a business. It uses OAuth, the same sign-in handshake behind "Log in with Google." An agent starts as a guest, able to check stock or a returns policy, and once the customer signs in it can get read-only or write access.

Businesses can route agents through their website, through APIs (MCP or OpenAPI) or through their own agent. Partners named include Genesys, Instinct, Rocket, Shopify, Stripe and Walmart. Version 0.1 of the spec is due later in October, and payments are a future extension. Stripe and Shopify also back Visa's rival Trusted Agent Protocol, so this is not settled. The report comes from one outlet, The Next Web, and the spec is not yet public.

On your desk: nothing to build yet, but it sketches how a family's own agent might someday ask Travis Wall Studio's Squarespace and Skool storefront about a coaching package or sign up for one.

Small French psilocybin study links inner dialogue to staying sober

Researchers at Nimes University Hospital and the University of Montpellier looked at 20 people with alcohol use disorder who received a 25 mg psilocybin dose. The analysis, in the Journal of Affective Disorders, covered what people said in follow-up integration sessions, the talks where people make sense of the experience.

Those still abstinent at three months more often described inner dialogue, rethinking familiar ways of behaving, and making commitments. Those who relapsed emphasized sensory and visual content. The authors call inner dialogue a possible mechanism to be tested in future studies, not a proven one.

This is a small, exploratory sample of 20, reported through PsyPost; we did not open the journal paper. One study so far, and it describes a pattern, not a cause. Science only, not health advice.

On your desk: it is a neat parallel to scoring your remote-viewing sessions, where what you say about a session, the structure versus the vivid imagery, may matter more than the imagery. Worth a column in your scoring sheet.
