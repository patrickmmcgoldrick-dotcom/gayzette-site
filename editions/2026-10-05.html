OpenAI's GPT-6 guide covers model sizes, caching and prompting

Three price tiers, a caching tip, and advice to stop over-explaining to the model.

On Friday, October 2, OpenAI published a how-to guide for building with GPT-6, its GPT-6 family of AI models. It covers choosing a model size, caching and how to prompt.

There are three tiers, priced per million tokens (a token is a small chunk of text, roughly a word or part of one). Per OpenAI, GPT-6 Astra costs $10 for input and $50 for output. GPT-6.1 Sol costs $2 and $10, and OpenAI describes it as "near-Astra intelligence for a fifth of the price". GPT-6 Luna costs $0.10 and $0.50.

Two other tips stand out. Prompt caching (reusing the part of a prompt that never changes) makes cached input tokens up to 95% cheaper than uncached ones, per OpenAI. The guide also says to match reasoning effort, from Low to Max, to the task. Eric Provencher of OpenAI put it this way: "Models have gotten much better at understanding nuance and ambiguity, so overly specific guidance can now hinder results where it previously helped"

On your desk: this paper runs every morning with the same standing instructions, which is the exact shape of job caching is meant for. Check whether any n8n or Claude automation repeats a long prompt daily, and try the cheapest tier on simple steps like sorting links. Then trim the over-detailed prompts.

On your desk Model size and caching are the two biggest dials on what an automation costs to run.

Anthropic puts $100M into training 10,000 Claude engineers

Anthropic announced Claude Frontier Academy on October 2: a $100M commitment to train 10,000 "Frontier Deployed Engineers" (engineers who help companies put Claude to work on real projects) by the end of 2027.

The program is a multi-day in-person session followed by a 12-week residency leading real Claude projects, with badges called "Claude Resident Engineer" and "Claude Frontier Deployed Engineer." The first cohort includes Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley and Novo Nordisk. Sites are San Francisco, New York and London. The Partner Network already counts 175,000+ certifications across 46,000 firms.

On your desk: this is aimed at enterprise engineers, not solo builders, so there is no homework. The useful signal is that big companies now pay for people who can run Claude projects start to finish, which is a fair description of what you do with n8n and Cowork.

OpenAI starts testing visual ads inside ChatGPT

OpenAI is testing a new visual ad format in ChatGPT, starting during image generation. Testing begins this month in the US with select advertisers. OpenAI says ChatGPT has 1.2 billion weekly users.

It also added Hightouch, Tealium and LiveRamp for conversion tracking (checking whether an ad led to a purchase), plus attribution and incrementality partners (tools that estimate whether an ad caused extra sales). Per OpenAI, WeightWatchers saw a 15.3% lower cost per acquisition than its blended paid-search benchmark (measured by DV Rockerbox), and 93% of visitors from ChatGPT Ads to Portland Leather were new. OpenAI says ads do not influence ChatGPT's answers.

On your desk: for Travis Wall Studio, these are company-reported results from advertisers, not a verdict. Nothing to do today, but note that image generation is where the ads start.

Google pauses its open-source bug bounty after a flood of AI reports

Google paused its Open Source Software Vulnerability Rewards Program, which pays people for finding security holes in open-source code, effective October 1, 2026, TechCrunch's Anthony Ha reports.

Google said: "This pause is due to a significant rise in automated submissions, the vast majority of which are not valid." Engineers and maintainers faced a flood of invalid reports containing AI-generated hallucinations (made-up details stated as fact). Google will give an update in Q1 2027, and participants were pointed to Google's other bug bounty programs.

On your desk: a lesson for any automation that sends things to other humans. If an n8n flow files reports, emails or posts, add a check that the thing is real before it goes out, because one unchecked bot can swamp a reader.

  • 01Nobel in Medicine goes to three optogenetics pioneers Mon Oct 5

    The 2026 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine went to Karl Deisseroth (Stanford), Peter Hegemann (Humboldt University) and Georg Nagel (University of Würzburg). Their work led to optogenetics, in which genes for light-sensitive proteins are put into chosen brain cells so pulses of light can switch them on and off. It is used to study memory, fear, anxiety, addiction and sleep, and clinical uses have partially restored vision in visually impaired patients.

    Seoul Economic Daily

  • 02Tiny umbrellas helped coral keep its color in a hot summer Sat Oct 3

    Dr. Karen Neely's team at Nova Southeastern University put 20 PVC-and-nylon shades over 10 brain coral colonies, cutting light by 60%, and kept 20 control corals for comparison. In a summer with the second-highest heat stress on record, the shaded corals "lost less color than expected," Good News Network reports of the study in Frontiers in Marine Science. Scaling up is the open problem.

    Good News Network

  • 03A one-in-100-million ghost lobster named Casper goes to college Sun Oct 4

    Casper, a white lobster from the Maine State Aquarium, is now at the University of New England's Arthur P. Girard Marine Science Center. Dr. Markus Frederich's team is analyzing more than 15,000 genes with noninvasive methods to explain odd lobster colors. The program began in 2024 with an orange lobster named Peaches.

    Good News Network

  • 04Australian home batteries now outpace rooftop solar installs Sun Oct 4

    Australian households installed more than 276,000 home batteries in the first half of 2026, up 52% year on year, compared with more than 180,000 rooftop solar installs. The Cheaper Home Batteries Program has produced 457,439 first-year systems and 12.9 GWh of storage. Batteries are now in about 1 in 7 Australian homes.
