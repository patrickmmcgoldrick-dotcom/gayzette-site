Anthropic API adds webhooks, user profiles and a web-fetch off switch

Two October 20 entries in the Claude Platform release notes add tracking and admin controls.

By The Gayzette, with Claude · 2 min read · Wonder ◆◆◆◇◇

Disclosure: Anthropic makes Claude, the model that helps make this paper. Two entries dated Oct 20 in the Claude Platform release notes add new controls to the Claude API. User profiles let you tell Anthropic which of your end customers each API request is for. Organization webhooks (automatic messages sent to an address you choose when something happens) arrive in the Admin API, in beta with the `webhooks-2026-10-07` beta header.

Org admins can also switch off web fetch, the tool that lets Claude read a web page, from the Console Capabilities page. With it off, the notes say, "a Messages API request that includes the web fetch tool fails with a 400 error," which is the web's way of saying the request was refused. The same notes put the Claude Tag API in beta for Enterprise orgs and move inference hooks out of beta for Enterprise.

On your desk: if any automation or client-facing tool calls the Claude API, user profiles let you attribute requests to end customers, and the new webhooks (beta; contact your Anthropic account team for access) notify you when Anthropic restricts or bans one of your user profiles. Check that none of your requests lean on web fetch before an admin flips that switch.

On our desk

It directly changes how Claude-powered automations can be tracked and controlled this month, and web-fetch requests can now fail by admin choice.

Meta releases Muse Spark Open, an 816B open-weight model

A giant model with downloadable weights, per its Hugging Face card.

Meta's Hugging Face model card (Oct 15) lists Muse Spark Open at 816B total parameters (the numbers a model learns) with 42B active at a time, a 1M-token context at inference (how much text it can read at once), and weights in BF16 and NVFP4, two ways of storing those numbers. The card says NVFP4 needs "at least 700GB of VRAM" (graphics-card memory) and BF16 about 2TB.

The license is the Muse Model License, which requires displaying "Built with Muse Spark Open." The card lists scores of 89.2 on MCP-Atlas, 80.4 on OSWorld Verified and 76.4 on Terminal-Bench 2.1. The caveat: it is nine days old, and we read the card but ran nothing, so treat the scores as the listing's numbers.

On your desk: not runnable at home, but an open-weight option on third-party hosts. If an n8n workflow ever swaps in a hosted model, the credit-line license term is the first thing to check.

Sage-A1.5: an open 35B agentic model under Apache-2.0

Read the brief

InternScience's Hugging Face card claims top scores among models of its size.

InternScience released Sage-A1.5, a 35B mixture-of-experts model (a design where only part of the model works on each word; Hugging Face metadata says 36B) under the Apache-2.0 license, built on Qwen3.6 and Agents-A1 for long-horizon agentic work, meaning many-step tasks. It supports subagent orchestration with isolated contexts, an opt-in verification mode and native tool use, with a 256K context window (512K with YaRN, a stretching technique).

Reported scores are SWE-bench Pro 61.2, BrowseComp 81.7 (82.8 with orchestration) and GAIA 95.1. The card says the model ranks first among comparable models on a broad range of tasks, though its own table has Agents-A1 ahead on GAIA. The caveat: those scores are self-reported, and the release date (about Oct 20) is inferred from the card's "Published 4 days ago."

On your desk: a small Apache-licensed agent model that could run locally for cheap automations. Try it on three of your own tasks before believing any leaderboard.

Spirit

LSD pushes brain rhythms faster, weaker and more complex

A Communications Biology study from Université de Montréal's CoCo Lab, via PsyPost.

Seventeen healthy volunteers got 75 micrograms of LSD by IV in one session and a saline placebo (a dummy) in another, 14 days apart, single-blind (volunteers did not know which). Alpha and beta are bands of rhythm in brain-wave recordings: alpha peak frequency rose about 2 Hz and beta about 5 Hz, yet their power still dropped. Two scores of how unpredictable a signal is, Lempel-Ziv complexity and Higuchi fractal dimension, rose, especially in the back visual regions.

Classifiers (programs trained to tell the sessions apart) managed over 82% accuracy from frequency shifts and over 76% from complexity measures, where chance is 50%. The caveat: 17 people is small, and the report says the music result "is exploratory and should not be over-interpreted."

On your desk: for Universe Year, fractal dimension is a math idea (how rough a shape is at every zoom) measured on a living brain, a nice bridge between your two study tracks.

More in Spirit

Chest vibration plus meditation tied to denser brain pathways

Read the brief

A Neuropsychopharmacology study from Emory University School of Medicine, via PsyPost.

Researchers including Negar Fani randomized 116 trauma-exposed adults with elevated dissociation (feeling detached from your body or surroundings). Half meditated with a sternum-mounted vibration device and half without, across eight brief mindfulness sessions. Only the vibration group showed increased nerve density in the corticospinal tract, a bundle of nerve fibers linking brain and body, and bigger gains in body awareness. Both groups reduced dissociation by similar amounts.

The caveat: this is an interim analysis of an ongoing trial, the outcomes were self-reported, and our source is a secondary report. This is a science note, not advice.

On your desk: for your consciousness reading, note that dissociation fell in both groups, so the vibration did not add there; what differed was a body-awareness score and a scan measure.

From the history: The Mowing-Devil (22 Aug 1678) is a five-page Hertfordshire pamphlet whose woodcut shows a devil mowing an oat field in concentric rings.
