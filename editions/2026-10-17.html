Claude Code 2.1.302 says standalone Claude Design closes by December 14

A small version bump carries one deadline and three useful settings, including a banner for failed routine runs.

The Claude Code changelog for version 2.1.302 (the running list of what changed in each release, dated October 16) adds a notice to two commands, /design-sync and /design-login. The notice says that standalone Claude Design, the separate site at claude.ai/design, closes by December 14, 2026. The changelog gives no detail about a replacement, so we cannot say what to move to. Treat it as a calendar reminder, not a plan.

Three other changes ride along. CLAUDE_CODE_AUTO_MODE_MODEL picks the model for the auto-mode classifier, the judge that decides whether an action may run without asking you; it affects the client side only. /config gains an "Idle compaction" row to switch off the automatic summarizing of idle sessions. And cloud sessions get a banner on a routine's page saying why its last run did not start, for example when an organization hits its limit on concurrent cloud sessions.

On your desk: the scheduled routines that build this very paper are exactly where that banner helps. If an edition ever fails to start, the routine's page should now say why, such as a concurrent-session limit. And if you or Travis use claude.ai/design, plan around mid-December.

On your desk A hard December 14 deadline plus a new way to see why a scheduled routine did not start.

GPT-6 Pro in ChatGPT can now split a task among parallel agents

OpenAI's ChatGPT release notes for October 16 list an update to GPT-6 Pro in the chat tab. It brings faster responses, better use of plugins and tools, and multiple agents that can work on parts of a task in parallel. Picture a kitchen where several cooks each take a dish.

The note says it is rolling out to eligible Pro users, so not everyone sees it yet. The entry is short, and it gives no benchmarks or details on how many agents run at once, so how much better it feels in practice is unknown.

ChatGPT adds Slides you can co-edit and present

The same OpenAI release notes announce Slides in ChatGPT. You can create presentations, edit them with teammates in real time (like a shared Google Doc, where everyone sees changes as they happen), and present or export them.

It is rolling out to eligible Pro, Business and Enterprise users who have Space access. A Space is a shared workspace inside ChatGPT, so some people on those plans may not have it yet. On the web, the notes say you can export to PowerPoint, PDF or Google Slides.

n8n 2.43.7 stops killing running jobs when a worker shuts down

n8n's GitHub releases page marks version 2.43.7, dated October 16, as the latest. Its one listed feature is "Suspend in-flight production executions at worker shutdown." A worker is the process that actually runs your workflows, and an in-flight execution is a run that has started but not finished.

Picture a cashier whose shift ends mid-sale: the old way, the sale might be dropped; the new way, it is paused so it can be picked up. The release line says suspend, and the page does not spell out how or where the run resumes, so check the notes before relying on it.

On your desk: if your n8n runs in queue mode (workers separate from the main app), restarts and deploys should now be less risky for long workflows.

Spirit

Small Oregon study tests cheaper group psilocybin for low-income depression

PsyPost reports on a Journal of Psychedelic Studies paper noting psilocybin therapy has mostly reached the well-off. Matthew Hicks and colleagues tested a group model inside Oregon's state-regulated program: two online prep sessions, two group dosing sessions a week apart, then online integration, talking the experience through afterward.

Participants had major depression and incomes at or below 200% of the federal poverty line. Twenty began, 19 finished. Attendance was 98%, no severe adverse events occurred, and satisfaction was 4.8 of 5. On the Hamilton Depression Rating Scale, the average went from 22.21 to 8.79 two days after the second dosing, and 11.89 at three months.

The caveat is big: this was a single-arm feasibility study, with no control group and no blinding. As PsyPost reports, it is not possible to say how much of the improvement came from psilocybin itself.

On your desk: a prompt for your consciousness reading about how group and setting may shape what a substance appears to do.

Under anesthesia, the brain's memory hub still tracks the words it hears

Baylor College of Medicine researchers recorded the hippocampus, a brain region tied to memory, in epilepsy-surgery patients under general anesthesia, using Neuropixels probes (very fine electrodes that record many single neurons at once). Per ScienceDaily, neurons responded differently to unexpected tones, and discrimination improved over the session, which is a sign of plasticity, the brain adjusting with experience.

During short stories, activity carried information about word category (nouns, verbs, adjectives) and predicted upcoming words. Senior author Sameer Sheth said, "This work pushes us to rethink what it means to be conscious."

Caveats: one anesthetic type, hippocampus only, and the authors say patients likely did not consciously understand the stories. The patient count was not stated in the article, and we read the release, not the Nature paper.

On your desk: it is a neat data point for your meditation questions. Processing something is not the same as experiencing it.
