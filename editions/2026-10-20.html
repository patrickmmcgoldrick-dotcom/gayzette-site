MiniMax says its small, fast model just got 3x faster

An updated M3.1 Flash promises cheaper long-document work, though the numbers are the company's own.

By The Gayzette, with Claude · 2 min read · Wonder ◆◆◆◇◇

MiniMax, a Chinese AI company, said on Oct 19 that it has updated M3.1 Flash, the small, fast model in its lineup. Reuters reports the company says the new version has nearly tripled its online inference speed and cut usage costs by 70%, with big gains in agentic tasks (where the AI works through steps on its own), programming and cybersecurity.

Think of it as swapping a sports car for a very quick bicycle: not the fanciest ride, but you can send a hundred of them on errands. Tech Insider lists the price at $0.15 per million input tokens (tokens are chunks of text, roughly three-quarters of a word) and says the model can read 1 million tokens at once. MiniMax's own docs page confirms that 1,000,000-token window but lists no prices.

The caveat is a real one: the speed and cost figures are MiniMax's own claims, the price comes from a secondary article, and the model is offered only through MiniMax's service, with no downloadable weights, license or date announced.

On your desk: a cheap long-context model is worth a test run in an n8n workflow that chews through bulk text, such as sorting a pile of Obsidian vault notes, where Claude-class prices add up. Try ten sample notes first and compare the results side by side.

On our desk

Cheap, fast models are what make always-on automations affordable, and affordability is what turns a clever demo into a daily habit.

Notion rebuilds its agents to run one task for a day

Notion's release notes describe 24-hour agent tasks, a cost-saving model router and bring-your-own-AI.

Notion's Oct 19 release notes, "Agents, rebuilt to do more for less," cover agent tasks that can run for up to 24 hours, an "Auto" model router, a new credit system and Bring Your Own AI.

A router is a traffic cop for questions: easy ones go to a cheaper model, hard ones to a pricier one, so you don't pay luxury rates for a grocery list. "Bring Your Own AI" means plugging your own model account into Notion instead of using only the ones it supplies.

The caveat: we confirmed the headline features on Notion's own page, but it gave us little detail beyond them, so how well a day-long task holds up is still untested by us.

On your desk: if a Cowork or Claude Code automation runs for hours, give it checkpoints, a saved log it can resume from, so a hiccup at hour nine doesn't erase hours one through eight.

DeepSeek ships a fresh V4.1-Flash update under the MIT license

Read the brief

A dated update to an open-weight model, reported by Tech Insider.

Tech Insider reports that DeepSeek released V4.1-Flash-1017 on Oct 17, an update to the previous Flash version. Citing DeepSeek's changelog, it says the changelog cites stronger benchmark performance, faster task completion and shorter outputs; Tech Insider adds that pricing and calling method are unchanged, though the changelog excerpt it quotes does not mention pricing.

"Open-weight" means the trained model's settings are published for anyone to download, like getting the recipe and not only the restaurant. Tech Insider says the weights are on Hugging Face under the MIT license, one of the most permissive, and the model is also on DeepSeek's API and NVIDIA NIM.

The caveat: no benchmark numbers were published, and the MIT claim rests on the secondary article. We did not open the changelog.

On your desk: if the API keeps a stable model name, this is a candidate backup step in an n8n workflow, for when your main model is down or rate-limited. Test it on non-sensitive text first.

Spirit

One psilocybin dose beat placebo early; by one year, the gap closed

A small Swedish trial in JAMA Network Open, reported by PsyPost, with a blinding problem.

PsyPost reports a double-blind trial in JAMA Network Open, led by Hampus Yngwe of Karolinska Institutet. Thirty-five adults with recurrent depression got one 25 mg psilocybin dose (17 people) or niacin (18), a vitamin that causes a flush and so serves as an "active placebo," plus five support sessions.

By day 42, remission was 53% in the psilocybin group versus 6% for placebo. By day 365 the difference was not statistically significant: 53% versus 41%, because the placebo group had climbed from 12%. Two psilocybin participants had severe anxiety lasting weeks.

The caveats are large: 35 people is small, and blinding largely failed, with about 94% of the psilocybin group guessing correctly, so expectation may explain part of the early gap.

On your desk: a clean case study for Universe Year-style testing. Write down the claim, control, sample size and one-year result, then check each against the headline. Both "beat placebo" and "gap closed" are true here.

More in Spirit

DEA proposes five more tryptamines for Schedule I; comments close Oct 23

Read the brief

A regulatory deadline this week, per HealingMaps' summary of the Federal Register notice.

Per HealingMaps, the DEA proposes putting five more tryptamines in Schedule I, the most restricted federal tier, for substances the agency says have no accepted medical use. They are 4-OH-DiPT, 5-MeO-AMT, 5-MeO-MiPT, 5-MeO-DET and DiPT (docket DEA1715). A tryptamine is a family of compounds sharing a chemical backbone; psilocin, psilocybin's active form, is another member.

Comments and hearing requests are due Oct 23, 2026, 11:59 p.m. Eastern. HealingMaps says this is a restart: the DEA proposed the same five in January 2022, withdrew, and asked HHS for a new review in November 2023. Psilocybin, MDMA, ketamine and 5-MeO-DMT are not in this rule.

The caveat: we read HealingMaps' summary, not the Federal Register notice. MAPS, an advocacy group, opposes the proposal, so weigh that voice as an interested party.

On your desk: if you follow this beat for the podcast, open the docket yourself before Oct 23 and note which compounds a claim actually names.
