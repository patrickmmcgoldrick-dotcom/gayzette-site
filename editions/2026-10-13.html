Meta launches two new AI models aimed at coding agents

Muse Wonder and Wonder Full reportedly score near the top of one outside scoreboard.

Meta released two new AI models on Monday. Muse Wonder is available through the Meta Model API and a coding tool called Muse Code; Wonder Full is limited to Muse Code subscribers. Meta advertises "Active Memory," which carries context across chats, and "Active Focus," which keeps the model working on hard goals.

On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3, a third-party scoreboard, run at maximum reasoning, Wonder scored 52 and Wonder Full 56, against 48 for Meta's earlier Muse Spark 1.3. One quoted post puts Wonder Full second overall, 2 points behind Claude Opus 5.5. A fair caveat: this rests on a single trade-press report (TestingCatalog), the benchmark numbers are reported rather than independently checked, and pricing and context window were not stated.

On your desk: this is a new coding-agent rival to Claude Code that reportedly sits near the top tier. It may be worth a look for your Claude Code and n8n builds once pricing is public.

On your desk A possible second engine for Patrick's Claude Code and n8n building, if the reported scores hold up.

The AI Builder · briefly

n8n adds Nano Banana image models to its Gemini node

n8n, the workflow-automation tool, shipped version 2.42.6 on October 9 and a bug-fix release, 2.42.7, on October 12. The first adds two image models, Nano Banana 2.1 and 2 Lite, to the Google Gemini node's model list (a node is one building block in a workflow). It also blocks creating or updating workflows that use deprecated nodes, meaning building blocks n8n has retired. Version 2.42.7 is fixes only, including resuming deferred tool calls for skill-loaded tools. On your desk: after updating, pick the Nano Banana models right in the node, and check whether any older workflows use deprecated nodes before the next save.

Google's Gemini 4 Argon shows up in limited tests

This is a test, not a launch. Google announced Gemini 4 Argon on September 30 with limited access, and users on X report spotting it in A/B tests, TestingCatalog says, (where different users randomly get different versions) on Antigravity. Google claims 77.9% on DeepSWE and first place on the Vals finance, legal and tax index and on Zapier's AutomationBench, though its own table trails Opus 5.5 on some coding tests; none of these figures are independently verified. Antigravity shows Argon-only context options of 256K, 512K and 900K tokens. A paid API and AI Ultra come next, with no date. On your desk: AutomationBench is the closest thing to an n8n-style workflow test, so if API access arrives, Argon could be a candidate for n8n's Gemini nodes.

  • 03Bowel cancer trial: no recurrences at 33 months after pre-surgery immunotherapy Wed Oct 7

    In a University College London trial called NEOPRISM-CRC, 32 patients with stage 2-3 bowel cancer of the MMR-deficient (MSI-high) type got up to nine weeks of an immunotherapy drug, pembrolizumab, before surgery. There were no recurrences at 33 months, and earlier data showed 59% had no detectable disease after treatment. It is a small trial with no control group, presented at an AACR meeting.

    ScienceDaily (University College London)

  • 04Australia's rooftop solar sets a record on Sunday Mon Oct 12

    On October 11, rooftop solar in Australia's National Electricity Market hit 17,488 MW, up 495 MW on the 16,993 MW record set September 20. Solar supplied 72.29% of the market's consumption that day, and grid batteries delivered about 19.2 GWh. The analysis is by Geoff Eldridge of Global Power Energy.

    Solar Now (RenewEconomy analysis)

  • 07Berlin's app lets residents adopt and water street trees Mon Oct 12

Residents adopt and water them, and Potsdam, Leipzig, Magdeburg and Paris's 17th arrondissement have launched versions from the same code. Berlin passed 39C (103F) this summer.

    Good News Network

  • 08Changing foot angle while walking eased knee arthritis pain in a trial Sun Oct 11

    In a year-long trial of 68 people, with a fake-treatment comparison group, walking with the feet angled in or out by 5 or 10 degrees, personalized to each person, eased knee arthritis pain, with relief between ibuprofen and a narcotic. MRIs also showed slower cartilage loss. The study was published in The Lancet Rheumatology in August and got fresh coverage this week; the training, which used a shin-worn vibration device, needs simplifying before clinics can use it.

    Good News Network

  • 09A KLM family flies together: daughter co-pilots, dad captains, mom crews Sat Oct 10

    Zoe de Bruijn, 22, flew as co-pilot on a KLM flight out of Amsterdam captained by her father Michel de Bruijn, a pilot since 1992, with her mother Marjan van Nooijen in the cabin crew. The flight was in August. Zoe entered the KLM Flight Academy at 17 and has flown with her father five times.

    Good News Network

  • 10Lost model of a papal statue by Trevi Fountain's sculptor found in a salvage yard Sat Oct 10

    A builder paid 300 pounds at a Wiltshire salvage yard for a 27-inch marble model of Pope Clement XII, believed to be by Pietro Bracci, sculptor of the Trevi Fountain statuary, who made it around the 1730s as a pitch for a giant bronze. Woolley & Wallis's Mark Yuan Richards called it "an exceptionally exciting discovery." The auction estimate is about $6,600-$13,300.
