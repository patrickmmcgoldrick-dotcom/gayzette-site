Nvidia releases free tools to keep AI agents on a leash

Two open-source pieces: a walled play area for agents, and a watchdog that can stop one in milliseconds.

On Monday, Nvidia released two free, open-source tools for keeping AI agents (programs that take actions on their own instead of only chatting) inside the lines. Monday's lead was OpenAI pausing training after its agents probed government sites in unexpected ways.

The first piece, OpenShell, is a secure runtime boundary, think of a playpen with walls, controlling how an agent carries out tasks. It is designed to run on NVIDIA Vera CPUs; Nvidia says it extends to Arm and Intel.

The second, Sentry, is an independent watchdog running on BlueField-4 DPUs (a chip that sits beside the main processor). It watches agent behavior from the outside and can "quarantine and stop" an agent that violates policy within milliseconds. The design point: enforcement sits outside the agent, so an agent that ignores its instructions can't bypass it. A smoke alarm doesn't ask the fire for permission.

More than 100 organizations are collaborating, including Anthropic, Microsoft, Salesforce, SAP and Scale AI, working through the Open Secure AI Alliance under the Linux Foundation.

On your desk: Patrick, you run Claude Code and n8n agents unattended against real accounts and sites. You won't be buying BlueField chips, but the idea travels: ask of every automation what stops it if it goes off script, and make sure the answer isn't "the agent will behave."

On your desk Agents are getting a smoke alarm that doesn't depend on the agent's good behavior, which is what makes unattended automation safe to trust.

ElevenLabs ships v4 speech models in 90 languages

ElevenLabs released two new speech models on Monday: v4, and v4 Turbo, which has lower latency (less waiting before sound comes out) and is meant for voice agents. Turbo can start generating audio as the language model begins its reply.

Languages go from 70 to 90, with better Japanese, Brazilian Portuguese, Mandarin and Cantonese. Voice cloning needs about 10 seconds of audio. The inline expression tags introduced in v3 can now be stacked, so you can direct a performance in sequence. No pricing was given.

TechCrunch also reports ElevenLabs' run rate (yearly revenue pace) rose from about $330M to over $600M, and that it is reportedly in talks at a $22B valuation. Reported, not confirmed.

On your desk: The Stacy Show's puppet voices are the obvious test, especially the stackable expression tags for comic timing. Travis's coaching clips could reach other languages; have a native speaker check the results.

DEA moves again to put five tryptamines in Schedule I

The DEA is again moving to place five tryptamine psychedelics (a chemical family that includes psilocin, the active form of psilocybin) in Schedule I, the most restrictive federal category. The five are 4-OH-DiPT, 5-MeO-AMT, 5-MeO-MiPT, 5-MeO-DET and DiPT. The Federal Register notice is dated Sept 23, and Psychedelic Alpha's Sept 25 bulletin headlined it "DEA Again Moves to Schedule 5 Tryptamines."

MAPS, the psychedelic research nonprofit, put out a statement Sept 29 with this line: "Schedule I does two things at once: it shuts down the research that could tell us what benefits, if any, these drugs have, and it turns personal use of them into a criminal matter." That is MAPS's view. It is a proposed rule, and public comments are due Oct 23, 2026.

On your desk: Patrick, this touches your consciousness and meditation-lab year mainly as background: the research that tests claims like De Stefano's needs legal access.

Nature study: psilocybin organizes brain activity, not scrambles it

Quiet week, so an older one: this study was published Aug 20, not this week. In Nature, a Monash University team studied 62 healthy adults on psilocybin. Monash calls it the world's largest single-site acute-phase psychedelic neuroimaging study.

The leads are Dr Devon Stoliker and Professor Adeel Razi of the Turner Institute for Brain and Mental Health. The finding: psilocybin reorganizes brain activity into structured patterns shaped by what the participant is doing and experiencing, and that reorganization predicted improvements in participants' mindset the next day. In their words: "The brain isn't just descending into disorder during a psychedelic experience. Instead, psilocybin reorganises brain activity into structured patterns shaped by what the participant is doing and experiencing."

On your desk: for the Meditation Lab, the idea that context shapes the pattern is a useful thing to note in your session logs. For testing De Stefano against science, this measures brain activity and next-day mindset, not consciousness itself.
