Claude Agents Uncover Novel CRISPR-Like Enzyme System

Anthropic's life-sciences research group ran about 950 Claude agents for 21 hours through 200,000 candidate enzymes and surfaced a previously unknown CRISPR-like system, with CRISPR pioneer Feng Zhang calling the finding intriguing.

The Gayzette · Wonder ◆◆◆◆◇

Anthropic's newly launched life-sciences research group set about 950 Claude agents loose for 21 hours — roughly 210 million tokens of work — scanning 200,000 reverse transcriptases (enzymes that copy RNA into DNA) for anything unusual. The agents narrowed that pool to 3,500 candidates, then to a short list of just 20 systems worth a closer look.

Among those 20, the agents flagged a previously uncharacterized bacteriophage (a virus that infects bacteria) enzyme family they're calling "array-associated reverse transcriptases," or ART — built with a CRISPR-array-like structure, the same repeating-sequence architecture CRISPR gene-editing tools are built on. A technical preprint accompanied the announcement, and CRISPR pioneer Feng Zhang called the finding "genuinely intriguing."

On your desk: it's the same many-agents-one-coordinator pattern behind your own n8n and Claude Code automations — a huge pool narrowed down to a short list — just running for a research team over a full day instead of a single build over an afternoon.

OpenAI's New Benchmark Grades Mental-Health Conversations

Read the brief

Per AI Weekly's report, OpenAI released MentalHealthBench, a benchmark of 1,215 synthetic mental-health conversations built with input from more than 80 psychologists across 22 countries. OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra scored 57.3% on it. It's a minor, filler-grade story rather than something directly actionable today, but it signals the industry is starting to grade models on how well they handle sensitive conversations, not just how smart they sound.

The Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) is seeking a sole-source contract for a commercial data subscription to the National UFO Historic Records Center (NUFOHRC), which DefenseScoop calls the "world's largest private collection of UAP-related historical records, including hundreds of thousands of unique case files."
