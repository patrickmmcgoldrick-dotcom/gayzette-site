Copilot Fusion picks the AI model for you, at half the cost

Fusion went generally available Oct 28 and routes each prompt between models, so you stop choosing one yourself.

By The Gayzette, with Claude · 2 min read · Wonder ◆◆◆◆◇

On Oct 28, GitHub made Copilot Fusion generally available. Instead of picking a model for each task, you choose Copilot Fusion and an effort level (Standard, High or Max), and it routes and orchestrates models for every prompt. It runs one of three workflows: Single (one model does the task), Cascade (a cheap model drafts, then a check accepts it or escalates to a stronger model), or Critique (one model drafts, another reviews, the first revises).

The headline number: on the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index, Fusion Max scored 60, within two points of Claude Code with Opus 5.5 Max. GitHub says it got there at about half the average API cost per task, with about half the tokens and about 40% less time. You pay each model at its standard rate, and paid Copilot plans get a limited-time 30% discount on model costs. The caveat: those figures come from GitHub, against one setup, and the changelog does not name which models Fusion routes between. Disclosure: Anthropic makes Claude, the model that helps make this paper.

On your desk: if your vault automations and Claude Code skills keep burning budget on one big model, Fusion is a cheap experiment. Select Copilot Fusion in the model picker and run one usual chore through it to compare cost.

ChatGPT Pro and Business subscribers can claim $100-$500 monthly API credits

OpenAI says paid ChatGPT plans now include a monthly API budget you claim yourself.

OpenAI announced that eligible ChatGPT Pro subscribers can claim $100, $200 or $500 a month in API credits, depending on their Pro tier. Business plans get $20 per paid Standard seat or $100 per paid Premium seat, pooled up to $500 per workspace. You claim the credits at chatgpt.com/settings/billing, then create an API key (the password a program uses to call the model) with the OpenAI Developers plugin in ChatGPT or Codex. OpenAI's announcement thread says the credits can be claimed every month.

The caveat is the fine print: the announcement does not define "eligible." Forum users report that App Store and Google Play subscribers do not see the claim option, and at least one $200 Pro user saw nothing to claim.

On your desk: if you already pay for ChatGPT Pro, check the billing page this week. A monthly API budget could fund a vault automation, or a small helper for The Stacy Show pipeline, at no extra cost.

GitHub Agentic Workflows go GA: automations written as plain Markdown

Read the brief

Describe a chore in English; a coding agent runs it inside GitHub Actions.

GitHub Agentic Workflows, an open-source project from GitHub Next, is now generally available. You describe an automation (issue triage, CI failure analysis, doc updates, test writing) in a natural-language Markdown file. It compiles to standard Actions YAML (GitHub's automation config), and a coding agent runs it inside GitHub Actions. The default engine is Copilot CLI, but you can bring your own agent and choose model providers per workflow.

Workflows run read-only by default in a sandbox behind an "Agent Workflow Firewall," and a threat-detection job scans proposed changes. It is available on GitHub Free, Team and Enterprise. The caveat: the default engine needs a Copilot license, the post gives no prices, and the 29 hours a week saved cited is one customer's testimonial (Accenture).

On your desk: this is the same move as writing a Claude Code skill, plain English as the program. Try one scheduled chore on your vault repo, and use `gh aw forecast`, which estimates monthly AI credit spend, before turning it on.

A mushroom that makes people see tiny humans has no known psychedelic

Utah researchers named the species behind "little people" visions; its active compound is still a mystery.

University of Utah researchers Colin Domnauer and Bryn Dentinger used DNA sequencing to identify Lanmaoa asiatica as the mushroom behind hallucinations reported in Southwest China ("Jian shou qing") and the northern Philippines ("Sedesdem"). Until now the species was thought to grow only in China. Chemical and genetic testing found no evidence of any known psychoactive compounds, including psilocybin. Domnauer estimates about 90% of people who have the visions describe elf-, clown- or fairy-like figures roughly 3 to 30 cm tall, often in the dozens or hundreds. The paper is in Mycologia, funded by the NSF.

Caveats: the active compound has not been isolated, so "new" rests on the absence of known compounds, and the 90% is the lead researcher's estimate, not a survey. This is science reporting, not medical advice.

On your desk: a fine thread for Consciousness University. Many people in different places report the same tiny figures, and nobody yet knows the chemistry. Sort that into the "unexplained, but being measured" pile.

High-speed train hits stopped train near Toledo, Spain; at least 68 dead

ConfirmedOn Wed Oct 28, near Urda in Toledo province on the Madrid-Seville high-speed line, a Renfe train from Madrid hit the back of a stopped train run by the private operator Iryo. Two carriages derailed. The Castilla-La Mancha regional government put the death toll at at least 68. Figures differ by source: regional government spokeswoman Esther Padilla said 397 passengers were on the Renfe train and 147 on the Iryo train; regional head Emiliano Garcia-Page said about 80 were injured, about 50 seriously and 15 critically; the regional government said 58 remained in hospital. ADIF suspended high-speed service between Madrid and Andalusia, and Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez declared three days of mourning, Oct 29-31. A crash on the same line nine months ago, involving the same two operators, killed 46.

Claimed by Transport Minister Oscar Puente, PM Pedro Sanchez, union spokesperson Joan RodriguezPuente says a computer fault in the safety system stopped trains along a 63 km stretch at 8:30 a.m.; the collision came at 8:53 a.m. after an "inexplicable" order from a central command center let the Renfe train restart while a stop signal was active, and he says the engineers were not to blame. He said, "I am not attributing responsibility to anyone, I am just stating the facts." Sanchez says the two crashes appear to have unrelated causes and that the government will investigate. Rodriguez questioned whether upkeep kept pace with opening high-speed service to private operators, while saying he was "not saying it was a determining factor."
