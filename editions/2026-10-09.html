Claude Haiku 5.5 arrives at one-tenth the old Haiku price

Anthropic's small, fast model drops to $0.10 per million input tokens.

The Gayzette · Wonder ◆◆◆◆◇

Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5 on October 7. Haiku is its small, quick model tier, and Anthropic calls this one "the cheapest, fastest, and most capable small model we've ever released." For prompts up to 100,000 tokens (a token is a chunk of text, roughly a word or part of one), it costs $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens. Haiku 4.5 was $1 and $5.

Anthropic says that is around 75% less to run on average. LLM Stats, an outside tracking site, adds two catches: a new tokenizer (the tool that chops text into tokens) makes the same text about 30% more tokens, and crossing 100,000 tokens raises the price of the whole request. LLM Stats also claims a 1M-token context window (how much text the model can read at once); Anthropic's page names no size. The scores are Anthropic's own, not independently tested: OSWorld 2.1 (offline subset, a computer-use test) 72.4% versus 15.7% for Haiku 4.5.

Anthropic pitches it for summaries, database queries and classification, as a helper beside Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5, which it says stay better at complex coding.

On your desk: this is the model to try for bulk n8n jobs, such as summarizing Plaud transcripts, sorting the studio inbox, or running the Gayzette's own scouts, where thousands of small calls add up. Test a batch against your current model before switching.

AI Builder

Sonnet 5.5 cache reads get cut in half

Alongside Haiku 5.5, Anthropic cut what Sonnet 5.5 charges for cache reads by 50%, from $0.20 to $0.10 per million tokens. A cache read is when the model reuses text it already saw earlier in a session instead of processing it fresh. Anthropic says this lowers Sonnet 5.5 costs on most agentic tasks (jobs where the model takes many steps on its own) by about 20%. Other Sonnet 5.5 prices are unchanged: $2 input, $10 output and $2.50 cache writes per million.

On your desk: long Claude Code and Cowork sessions keep rereading the same vault context, so this should trim the cost of those without you changing anything.

More in AI

Liquid AI releases open models that answer in one pass

Read the brief

Liquid AI released two open-weight models (the trained files are free to download), d1-3B and d1-omni-600M. They "produce an answer in a single forward pass" rather than writing out tokens one by one. d1-3B handles text and images; the 600M takes text plus images or audio and is called an "early research release." Liquid reports d1-3B answers in 8 ms per question on an RTX 4090 graphics card and 50 ms on a Jetson Orin Nano, and scores 48.57 on its Decision Index v0.2.1 (the company's own test). The page says "Download, fine-tune, and deploy without restrictions" but names no license, so check the model cards on Hugging Face.

On your desk: a tiny local classifier could tag and route Skool, Squarespace and email items in n8n with no API spend.

GLM 5.3 Fast claims faster output and a 1M window

Read the brief

A model-listing site, LLM Gateway, lists GLM 5.3 Fast as "a faster version of GLM-5.3" from Z.AI, reportedly 1.5 to 2 times faster at output. To be plain: this comes from that listing site only, and no Z.AI page confirming it was found. The listing also calls it "GLM-5.3-Prime" in one place. It shows a context of up to 1,000,000 tokens and pricing through one provider, SCX.ai, of $2.80 input, $0.56 cache read and $8.80 output per million tokens. On LLM Gateway, tool calling and structured JSON output are not supported.

On your desk: nothing to act on yet; wait for Z.AI to confirm it, and note that without tool calling on LLM Gateway it can't drive tool-using n8n agents there.
