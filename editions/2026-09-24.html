Amazon Lets Outside AI Agents Run Seller Tools

Amazon's Seller Assistant, itself built on Claude, now plugs straight into Claude with no code, and sellers are accepting its advice more than nine times out of ten.

By The Gayzette, with Claude · 1 min read · Wonder ◆◆◆◆◇

Amazon just opened up a new way to plug AI straight into a real business: a beta plugin connects its Seller Assistant tool — itself built on Claude through Amazon's cloud service Bedrock — directly into Claude, letting outside sellers link their listings, inventory, performance and sales data with no coding required. Setup takes about 60 seconds, and Amazon says sellers are accepting the tool's recommendations more than 90% of the time.

Anthropic's field CTO (a technical lead who works hands-on with outside companies), Eric Burns, said "Amazon Seller Assistant already uses Claude models to help sellers make informed, strategic decisions." One beta tester, James Oliver of Atlas Bar, put it simpler: "What took an afternoon now happens instantly."

It's a small beta today, but it's a template: a real operations dataset, wired straight into Claude, doing the boring analysis so a human just has to approve or ignore it.

Google's New AI Voices Speak 100+ Languages

Google released Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS (text-to-speech) and a lighter Flash-Lite version, covering more than 100 languages and dialects with over 2,000 ready-made voices, plus the option to design a brand-new voice just by describing it in a text prompt. It can clone a voice from a 30-second sample, with consent verification built in, and watermarks everything it generates with SynthID so the audio stays traceable as AI-made. You can direct pacing, emotion and dialect line by line. It's rolling out now to Google's AI Studio, the Gemini API, Gemini Notebook and Vids, with an enterprise version "coming soon." On Hume AI's quality index, the two new models ranked #1 and #2 overall.

On your desk: worth testing alongside Suno as a cheap, multilingual voice layer for narration or dance-coaching content on Travis Wall Studio.

YouTube Lets You Build Your Own Feed

Read the brief

YouTube announced Custom Feeds at its "Made on YouTube" event: you describe, in plain language, what you want to watch, and Google's Gemini assembles a personalized homepage tab out of it. It sits alongside the regular algorithmic feed rather than replacing it, and starts rolling out to web and mobile "beginning next month." YouTube's VP of Product Management framed the problem it's solving as navigating "over 20 billion videos in the YouTube corpus."

On your desk: once it's live, this is a new discovery door for Travis Wall Studio's dance content — a prompt-built feed could route a niche dance audience there more directly than the main algorithm does.

Remote-Viewing Group's Conference Returns To Dayton

The International Remote Viewing Association (IRVA), a nonprofit dedicated to what its own site calls "the responsible development and application of Remote Viewing," is bringing its annual conference back to Dayton, Ohio this October. Exact dates aren't set yet — the conference site says "CONFERENCE INFO COMING SOON!" and that full details "will be announced in the coming months" — but the program promises "expert presentations, hands-on workshops, collaborative remote viewing sessions," under a tagline calling it a place "where science meets spirit and community thrives." IRVA also runs an active research arm and a grant program for remote-viewing research, per its site.

On your desk: IRVA is about as close to a professional home base as CRV (controlled remote viewing, the structured protocol Patrick is studying) gets — its conference and research grants are worth having on the calendar once firm dates land.
