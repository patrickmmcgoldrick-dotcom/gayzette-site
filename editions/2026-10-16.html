Fast mode for Claude Opus 5.5 is now open on the API

Anthropic took it out of research preview, so nobody has to ask for access anymore.

By The Gayzette, with Claude · 1 min read · Wonder ◆◆◇◇◇

Yesterday, Anthropic's platform release notes said fast mode for Claude Opus 5.5 has left "research preview," which is the label for a feature that's still being tested with a limited group. You no longer need to request access.

Two small changes come with it. Requests used to carry a beta header, a little label a program attaches to say "I'd like the experimental version." For Opus 5.5 that label, `fast-mode-2026-02-01`, is no longer needed, though requests that still send it keep working. Opus 5.5 also now has a default fast-mode rate limit, meaning a built-in cap on requests per minute, in the Start, Build and Scale tiers. Fast mode for Opus 5 and Opus 4.8 is still in preview and still needs the header, and Opus 5.5 fast mode also runs on Claude in Microsoft Foundry for Azure deployments. One caveat: the page doesn't say what fast mode costs or how much faster it is, so check before you flip it on everywhere.

On your desk: any n8n Anthropic node in Travis Wall Studio's automations, or a Gayzette script, can ask for fast Opus 5.5 without a permission request first.

Claude Code 2.1.301 adds 37 fixes and a few guardrails

The update changes how long unattended runs wait, how models switch, and what the Read tool opens.

Claude Code 2.1.301 landed yesterday with 37 fixes in total. A new setting, `CLAUDE_CODE_RETRY_WATCHDOG_MAX_USAGE_LIMIT_WAIT_MS`, lets the unattended retry mode (which keeps retrying while nobody watches) give up when a usage limit won't reset for a long while, instead of waiting it out. Switching to a model with a smaller context window (a shorter conversation memory) now has the previous model summarize a chat that's too long to fit. The Read tool no longer reads binary files such as .xlsm, .parquet, .epub and .heic as text, and Grep and Glob now report an error instead of "no matches" when the search program itself fails. VS Code gains LaTeX rendering.

On your desk: if any overnight Claude Code job feeds the Gayzette, the new wait limit means it can stop cleanly rather than hang, and a search error is now visible instead of looking like an empty result.

Spirit

A drug-free trance practice blurred self and surroundings in a small study

Seventeen practitioners rated their deepest trance as far less "me" and far more "everything."

Researchers (De Oliveira, Meunier, Breton and Dambrun) interviewed 17 people trained in auto-induced cognitive trance about 15 months earlier, practicing about 10 minutes several times a week. On 0 to 100 scales, comparing their last meal with their most intense deep trance, sense of authorship over thoughts fell from 67.4 to 12.1 and motor agency from 83.8 to 10.4. Oneness with surroundings rose from 28.4 to 75.9, happiness from 50.5 to 74.2 and inner peace from 44.3 to 74.7. Only two links to happiness held up: feeling less at the center of the scene, and a sense of self less tied to one point. The caveat: it's a tiny pilot, everyone was trained by one institute whose affiliate is a co-author, and recalling a peak trance against a meal likely exaggerates the contrast.

On your desk: it's a prompt for your meditation practice: notice whether "feeling less at the center" is the part that feels good.

More in Spirit

Psilocybin, LSD and 2C-B leave different brain fingerprints in rats

Read the brief

Glucose scans showed each drug lighting up different regions, and only psilocybin left a lasting trace.

Gudmundsen and colleagues (Palner senior author) gave 48 rats psilocybin, LSD, 2C-B or saline, then ran three FDG-PET scans, which track glucose use as a stand-in for brain activity, at baseline, during the drug and a week later. Acute psilocybin raised relative glucose use in the dorsal striatum and insula. LSD lowered it across much of the cortex and raised it in midbrain, amygdala and hypothalamus. 2C-B lowered frontal cortex activity and raised midbrain dopamine-related areas. A week later, psilocybin left higher insula metabolism and a stronger insula-prefrontal link, the only network change that survived statistical correction; most LSD effects had faded. The caveat: it's an animal study of relative, not absolute, metabolism with no behavior measured, and Palner discloses shares in Compass Pathways and ATAI.

On your desk: for your Universe Year biology notes, it's a clean example of one chemical target producing different, measurable patterns.
