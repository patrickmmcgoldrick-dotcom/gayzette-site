Google ships gemini-api, one command-line tool for coding agents

A beta, zero-dependency client that handles images, audio, video, transcription and agent runs from a single binary.

By The Gayzette, with Claude · 1 min read · Wonder ◆◆◆◇◇

Google released gemini-api on Oct 27, a command-line client (a program you run by typing commands) now in beta. The page calls it "a stateless, zero-dependency command-line client" for AI coding agents, scripts and CI pipelines (automated build-and-test runs). One binary, installed by curl, PowerShell or `go install`, covers image, tts (text to speech), music, video, generate, analyze (PDFs, images, audio, video, CSV and public YouTube URLs) and transcribe, plus managed-agent commands such as agent run, status and cancel. It needs only a Gemini API key.

The caveats are Google's own. It is an open-source beta, and "breaking changes can occur between minor versions," so pin a release in CI. Inline file inputs cap at 14 MB raw, Files API uploads auto-delete after 48 hours, it is not an interactive coding agent, and the page gives no pricing.

On your desk: a Claude Code or Cowork automation could shell out to one command to transcribe a recording or analyze a video, with no SDK code to write. Try it on one small vault chore first.

Claude Code 2.1.312 stops overwriting big CLAUDE.md files

The Oct 27 release also fixes skills going missing after a cd, and caps hooks at 24.

Disclosure: Anthropic makes Claude, the model that helps make this paper. Claude Code 2.1.312, released Oct 27, fixed a bug where Claude could overwrite a very large CLAUDE.md (the instructions file it reads at the start) or an @-included file after reading only the start of it.

It also fixed per-directory and `paths:` skills (packaged instructions Claude loads for certain folders) not being found after a Bash `cd` into a repo subdirectory. And it capped prompt and agent hooks (automatic actions that run at set moments) at 24 running at once per session, subagents included. The caveat: our link is the releases list page, not a version-specific page.

On your desk: with the big instruction files in your vault, the overwrite fix is the one to be glad of. If you run many hooks in one session, check you are under 24.

More in AI

Ling 3.1 Flash: an MIT-licensed 560B open model with a 1M-token window

Read the brief

Priced at $0.30 in and $0.90 out per million tokens; the benchmark score is quoted from the vendor's model card.

inclusionAI published Ling-3.1-flash-1019 on Hugging Face. It is a mixture-of-experts model (many specialist sub-networks, only some used per word) with about 560B total and roughly 25B active parameters, a 1M-token context window (how much text it reads at once) and an MIT license, meaning open reuse. The model card reports a score of 44 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v4.3.2, up from 41 for its predecessor. Latent Space lists pricing of $0.30 in and $0.90 out per million tokens. Weights are on Hugging Face; the card also links ModelScope and OpenRouter pages.

The caveats, from the model card: the benchmarks are vendor-reported, the example server commands point to the base `Ling-3.1-flash` path rather than the `-1019` variant, vLLM needs `--trust-remote-code`, and the example runs on 8xH200 GPUs.

On your desk: a cheap hosted option through OpenRouter for long-context bulk jobs. Self-hosting is data-center territory.

Liquid AI's LFM3 fits a 1.7B model in about 1 GB

Read the brief

Open weights built for phones and laptops; every speed claim is Liquid's own.

Liquid AI released LFM3, six open-weight models of roughly 2B parameters each (small enough for a phone). The LFM3-1.7B card lists a 256K-token context, 16 languages, about 1 GB at 4-bit (compressed numbers), and GGUF, MLX and ONNX versions; Latent Space says the text model is also on Ollama. Liquid's tests on an iPhone 17 Pro, Apple M5 Max and Ryzen AI Max+ 395 claim at least 2.5x faster prefill (reading the prompt) and 1.8x faster decode (writing the answer) than comparison models.

The caveats are on the card. It is "not recommended for agentic coding or complex multi-step reasoning due to its small size," it scores 43.40 on SWE-bench Verified against 51.20 for Qwen3.5-9B, all numbers are Liquid's own, and the license is the LFM Open License v1.0, not Apache or MIT.

On your desk: a free on-device model for private jobs like cleaning up transcripts or tagging notes.

Spirit

Psilocybin lowered inflammation markers in 12 people with Parkinson's

A small open-label pilot in JAMA Psychiatry, reported Oct 21; a starting point, not proof.

This is a small open-label pilot (everyone knew they were getting the drug) of 12 people with Parkinson's disease and mood problems. They got psilocybin in two sessions. Five blood markers of inflammation were significantly lower afterward and stayed lower a month after the last dose. A marker of mitochondrial stress (strain in the cell's power plants) improved right after dosing but not at longer follow-up.

There was no control group, so this is a starting point, and the authors say follow-up studies are needed. Marijuana Moment notes UCSF and Columbia ran it, funded by Silo Pharma and an anonymous donor. This is science reporting, not medical advice or a treatment claim.

On your desk: a tidy receipt for the Consciousness University thread that the field is moving from how psychedelics feel to what they measurably do in the body.
